How to strengthen your CV and LinkedIn: a step-by-step guide for developers

By: Zakhar Garan · 26 November 2026

I compiled this guide with the help of AI from many sources, tips and instructions. Check every point against your own facts.

You can hand the guide to an AI: copy the text into a chat and ask it to help with your profile. First collect your facts (dates, job titles, numbers, stack, education), then ask it to check the sections one by one. Do not let the AI invent experience or numbers you never had.

1. Collect all your facts in one document

Before you write anything, build a table: positions with dates, companies, numbers (releases, users, load), stack, education, certificates. This is your fact sheet.

There is one rule: the same truth everywhere. LinkedIn, your CV, Djinni, Upwork and your emails must match in dates, titles and years of experience. A recruiter notices discrepancies quickly, and trust in the rest of your profile drops.

2. Look at your profile through a recruiter’s eyes

A recruiter usually looks at a profile for only a few seconds. In that time they need to understand:

  • who you are (headline);
  • what you have worked with (the first two lines of About);
  • what you did and at what scale (Experience).

If the answers to these questions are not obvious, rewrite.

3. Headline

The formula: role + level + technologies + what sets you apart.

An example of the structure: Senior iOS Engineer | iOS Tech Lead | Swift, UIKit | [your topic]

Do not write “passionate developer” and similar phrases with no content. And check your headline after any change: when you add a new position, LinkedIn may replace it on its own with “title at company”.

4. About

Three paragraphs:

  • Who you are: role, years of experience, industry.
  • Your strongest points: one or two achievements with context and result, not a list of everything.
  • What you are looking for: role, work format, how to reach you.

5. Experience: action, context, result

Bad: “Responsible for developing new features”.

Better: “Led the development of SDUI components together with the Android team, designers and product managers, and wrote documentation for developers”.

  • Write only what you can explain in an interview. If you were a Tech Lead without direct reports, say so: “Tech Lead on a client project”.
  • Do not invent scale. “Led a team of 10+” without proof becomes a problem in the very first conversation.
  • For projects under NDA, describe the features and the scale, but do not name the client.
  • Give only numbers you can explain: where they come from and how they were measured. If a number belongs to the whole team, write “helped achieve”, not “delivered”.

6. Skills

Keep what you actually use. Skills you only studied are better left out. A few real technologies are more useful than a long list of forty names.

7. Certificates

Keep certificates that relate to your role. Random courses from other fields are better removed: they distract from the main point.

8. Open to work

Choose roles and locations that really suit you, and list only the formats you are ready to consider (remote, hybrid, on-site). You can leave recruiters a short private note with your preferences.

9. CV

  • A 3-4 line summary: years of experience and a few concrete numbers.
  • Your strongest experience should be on the first page. If it is hidden on the second, swap the blocks.
  • Give links as full URLs, not just labels.
  • Check the PDF carefully: the footer, job title and dates must match LinkedIn.
  • Easy Apply and some job sites may require an anonymous version without the names of companies you do not want to show.

10. Recommendations and activity

  • Ask for 2-3 recommendations from colleagues who can confirm your role and results. The ones with a specific example work best.
  • Add 2-3 items to Featured: a link to a product you built, or a post with results.
  • Write about your work. Complaints about former employers and jokes on that topic look like irritation.

11. Check everything once more

  • Dates, company names, years of experience, degree and job titles must look the same in every channel.
  • If an AI wrote any of the texts, reread every line. An AI can invent details, numbers or skills you never had.

12. How to work with AI

  • Give it a document with your facts and ask it to use only those.
  • Ask for a critical review, not just rewriting. It helps when the AI reads your profile like a recruiter, without your context.
  • Do not ask it to invent experience you did not have, even “as an example”.
  • The last step: read every sentence and ask yourself whether you can explain it in an interview.

Checklist

  • There is a fact sheet with dates, numbers, stack and education
  • The headline contains role, level and technologies
  • About has three paragraphs and concrete examples
  • Every Experience item has context and a result, and you can explain it
  • Skills contain only what you use
  • Certificates relate to your role
  • Open to work matches your real preferences
  • Your CV and LinkedIn do not contradict each other
  • You have 2-3 recommendations that confirm your role
  • Links and the PDF are checked
